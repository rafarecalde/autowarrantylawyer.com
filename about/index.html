Why We Take These Cases on Contingency

Florida's Motor Vehicle Warranty Enforcement Act allows a prevailing consumer to recover reasonable attorney fees and costs from the manufacturer (Fla. Stat. § 681.112(2)). That statutory framework is what makes a contingency practice in this area economically viable.

If the applicable Lemon Law allows recovering attorney’s fees from the manufacturer in addition to your recovery, we pursue that route. Otherwise the contingency fee is 30% of the gross recovery. That fee is based on the gross recovery and is not reduced by lease or loan payoffs, mileage offsets, use deductions, negative equity, or similar items. If we don't recover for you, you owe no attorney fee. There is no small upfront attorney fee and no retainer. Court costs and expenses, if any, are disclosed in the engagement — they are not a retainer.

Why Florida-Only

Every state has its own lemon law framework, with different rights periods, different repair-attempt thresholds, different arbitration requirements, and different fee-shifting provisions. We practice only in Florida because that's where we have the depth of experience, the relationships with the state-certified arbitration programs, and the courtroom familiarity that produces consistent outcomes for clients. If you are a Florida resident or your vehicle was purchased or leased in Florida, we can help.

Why New Vehicles Only

The Florida Lemon Law applies only to new motor vehicles. Used vehicles fall outside Chapter 681. Federal warranty statutes (the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act) can sometimes apply to used vehicles with remaining warranty coverage, but the remedies are different, the fee-shifting is different, and the economics are different. We limit our practice to qualifying new vehicles so we can deliver the best outcome for the clients whose cases we accept.
