How a Florida Lemon Law Case Works

Contingency only. No recovery, no fee. Manufacturer pays the attorney fees.

The Florida Lemon Law is written to make it economically viable for ordinary consumers to take on vehicle manufacturers. The statute (Fla. Stat. § 681.112(2)) allows a prevailing consumer to recover reasonable attorney fees and costs from the manufacturer. That framework is what makes it possible for us to take qualifying cases on pure contingency — no retainer, no hourly billing, and no fee unless we recover for you.

Here is exactly how the process works, from the day you start a case review to the day the manufacturer cuts the check.

Step 1 — Case Review

You start a case review. We’ll ask for the original delivery date first. Florida’s Lemon Law rights period generally ends 24 months after that date (Fla. Stat. § 681.102(9)). If you’re clearly past that window — more than about 26 months after delivery — we say so before asking for more facts. If the date falls in the 24-to-26-month band, we still collect the facts for attorney review. A defect first reported during the rights period can be taken to the state arbitration board for up to 60 days after the period ends (Fla. Stat. § 681.109(4)). If you’re inside the 24-month window, we collect vehicle and contact details, a short repair history, and you submit.

The review is free. If the date is clearly past the filing window, we stop there. If it looks in-window, or it needs attorney review because it is in the 24-to-26-month band, you submit the facts first. After that, you can upload your document packet.

Step 2 — Document Packet

Before any engagement agreement goes out, send the full packet — email to rafael@recaldelaw.com or upload what you have:

  • Driver’s license
  • Vehicle registration
  • Lease or purchase contract
  • Repair tickets / repair orders

We do not send an engagement letter until this packet is in.

Step 3 — Fees and Engagement

Once the packet is in, we’ll tell you plainly whether the matter looks viable and how fees work. If the applicable Lemon Law allows recovering attorney’s fees from the manufacturer in addition to your recovery, we pursue that route. Otherwise the contingency fee is 30% of the gross recovery — not reduced by lease or loan payoffs, mileage offsets, use deductions, negative equity, or similar items. You do not pay an upfront attorney fee. If we don't recover for you, you owe no attorney fee. Court costs and expenses, if any, are disclosed in the engagement — not as a retainer.

If you want Recalde Law Firm to represent you, reply “I want to proceed.” We’ll send the engagement agreement for electronic signature. After it’s signed, we open the file.

Step 4 — Statutory Notice to the Manufacturer

Florida law requires the consumer to provide the manufacturer written notice of the nonconformity and a final opportunity to repair. This notice has to be sent correctly — registered mail, return receipt requested, or express mail, addressed to the specific corporate location designated by the manufacturer in its warranty materials. We draft and send the notice, track the response deadline, and document the final repair opportunity.

Step 5 — Arbitration or Civil Action

If the manufacturer's final repair opportunity fails — or if they refuse to honor their Lemon Law obligations — we move to a state-certified arbitration program (most commonly BBB AUTO LINE) or, if no program is available, to the Florida New Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board. For claims outside the arbitration framework or where arbitration fails, we file in civil court.

At every stage, we prepare the case as if it will be tried. That preparation — documentation, expert analysis, and a clear narrative of the repair history — is what produces serious settlement offers. Manufacturers respond to representation that is ready to go the distance.

Step 6 — Recovery

Under Florida law you are entitled to elect between:

  • A replacement with a comparable new motor vehicle acceptable to you; or
  • A refund of the full purchase price, including taxes, title, registration, optional equipment, and finance charges, minus a reasonable offset for mileage driven before the defect was first reported.

Once a recovery is agreed — whether through arbitration decision, settlement, or civil judgment — the manufacturer issues payment and takes the vehicle back. You move on.

Our Fee Promise

No recovery, no fee. If the applicable Lemon Law allows recovering attorney’s fees from the manufacturer in addition to your recovery, we pursue that. Otherwise the contingency is 30% of the gross recovery, not reduced by payoffs, mileage offsets, use deductions, or negative equity.

There is no small upfront attorney fee and no retainer. Court costs and expenses, if any, are disclosed in the engagement — they are not a retainer. We will not charge you personally for costs on an unsuccessful case.

What You Actually Do During the Case

Honestly — very little. You continue using the vehicle normally. You send us new repair orders as they accumulate. You let us handle communications with the manufacturer and the dealer. You don't have to surrender the vehicle, and you don't have to stop driving it. The case is designed to run in the background of your life while you get value from the vehicle you paid for.
